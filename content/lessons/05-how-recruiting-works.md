# How finance recruiting works: clubs, timelines, early programs and superdays
*Comes up in:* Every recruiting conversation, as "Where are you in the process?" and "What else are you applying to?" Timing is where most students lose out.

## Cold open
"Walk me through where you are in recruiting and what you're applying to."

Answer it out loud before you read on.

## The big idea
Finance recruiting runs earlier than almost any other field. At many large banks, the junior-year summer internship, which is the main route to a full-time offer, gets filled more than a year before it starts. So freshman and sophomore years are when you build what you'll be judged on: club experience, technical skills, a network and good stories. It's like a sports season where tryouts happen a year before the first game.

## How it works
Here's the typical path for investment banking. Other seats are similar but often later, and every firm is different.

| When | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Freshman fall | Finance clubs recruit, often with applications and interviews | Get into one or two clubs and start learning technicals |
| Freshman spring to sophomore fall | Early insight, discovery and sophomore programs open | Apply widely and start talking to alumni |
| Sophomore winter to spring | Junior-summer applications open at many large banks | Be interview-ready on fit and technicals |
| Junior summer | The internship itself | Perform well and earn the return offer |

A typical process has five stages: an application (resume, sometimes a cover letter), an online test or recorded video interview, a first round of about 30 minutes with fit and technical questions, a **superday** of three to six back-to-back interviews, and an offer, often with a short deadline to accept.

## In the real world
The calendar has moved earlier over the past decade, which people call recruiting creep. In recent cycles, several of the largest banks opened junior-summer internship applications in the winter or early spring of sophomore year, roughly 15 to 18 months before the internship started. Private equity firms have also pulled their own recruiting earlier, which pushes banks to lock in analysts sooner. The exact dates change every year, so put your career center's calendar and the careers pages of your target firms on a weekly check, and set alerts where firms offer them.

## The interview trap
The trap is either not knowing your own timeline or sounding scattered, applying to everything with no focus. Interviewers want to see a plan.

> I'm a sophomore focused on asset and wealth management. Right now I'm applying to sophomore and early insight programs at a few firms, and I'm getting ready for junior-summer applications, which open as early as this winter at some banks. In parallel, I'm on the research team of my school's investment club, where I've pitched two stocks, and I've had coffee chats with five alumni to learn how the seat works at different firms. My goal is to be fully interview-ready on technicals by early next year, so I'm working through accounting and valuation now.

## Your line
"In finance, the calendar is a skill: the students who land offers usually started a year earlier than they thought they needed to." **Follow-up you'll get:** "What other industries are you recruiting for?" Be honest, but make finance your clear focus. **The other side:** Some seats, including wealth management, corporate finance and many smaller firms, hire on a normal timeline, so a late start isn't fatal.

## Your turn
1. **Say it out loud:** Answer "Where are you in recruiting?" for yourself in 45 seconds.
2. **Rapid fire:**
   a. What's a superday?
   b. What's usually the main route to a full-time offer at a bank?
   c. Name two things you can do as a freshman that recruiters will notice later.
3. **Rewind:** What are the four steps of a 60-second markets answer?
4. **Brain teaser:** How many gas stations are there in the United States? Walk through an estimate.

## Answers
- **Say it out loud:** A strong answer gives your class year and target seat, what you're applying to now and when the next windows open, concrete preparation (a club role, technical study, a number of alumni conversations), and sounds calm and specific.
- **a.** The final round: several back-to-back interviews in one day, usually three to six, mixing fit and technical questions.
- **b.** A return offer after the junior-year summer internship.
- **c.** Any two of: getting into and contributing to a finance club, an early insight program, a relevant job or project, steady technical study, and real conversations with alumni.
- **Rewind:** The theme, the driver with numbers, the implication for prices, and what you're watching next with your view.
- **Brain teaser:** One path: about 250 million vehicles, each filling up about once a week, is about 35 million fill-ups a day. If a station handles about 250 fill-ups a day, that's roughly 140,000 stations. The commonly cited figure is around 145,000 to 150,000, but the clear logic matters more than the exact number.
