Lesson library

40 lessons on what first rounds actually ask

Each lesson opens with an interview question to answer before you read, then the idea in plain English, a worked example, the trap most candidates fall into, and questions to answer out loud. Read them in any order, or build a plan and get them in the right order for your dates.

Careers & fit

  1. The map of finance: sell side vs. buy side, and where every seat fitsAlmost every first conversation, as "What do you know about the industry?" or "Why this seat over the others?"
  2. "Tell me about yourself": turning your resume into a 60-second storyNearly every interview, usually as the first question, and it sets the tone for the next 30 minutes.
  3. Your "why finance" story: an answer only you could giveEvery first round and most club interviews, usually alongside "why this seat" and "why our firm."
  4. "What's going on in the markets?": a daily habit and a 60-second answerMost first rounds, especially asset management, wealth management and sales & trading, as an opener or a closer.
  5. How finance recruiting works: clubs, timelines, early programs and superdaysEvery recruiting conversation, as "Where are you in the process?" and "What else are you applying to?" Timing is where most students lose out.
  6. Investment banking: what bankers do, and bulge brackets vs. elite boutiques vs. middle marketEvery banking interview ("Why banking?", "Why a boutique over a bulge bracket?") and useful context for any seat.
  7. Telling stories that prove it: the STAR method and your story bankEvery behavioral question ("Tell me about a time...") in every seat, usually one to three per interview.
  8. Behaviorals, part 1: strengths, weaknesses and "tell me about a failure"Most first rounds and superdays. "Greatest weakness" and "tell me about a failure" are among the most common questions in any interview.

Accounting

  1. The three financial statements: what each one tells youNearly every technical first round, as "Walk me through the three financial statements."
  2. The income statement, line by lineAccounting technicals in every seat, and any time you discuss a company's earnings.
  3. The balance sheet: why assets always equal liabilities plus equityAccounting technicals, and any interview about a bank, an insurer or a company with heavy debt.
  4. The cash flow statement: why profit isn't the same as cashAccounting technicals in every seat, and especially in asset management and credit interviews.
  5. How the three statements link togetherBanking and private equity technicals, and every financial modeling test.
  6. PP&E, capex and depreciationAccounting technicals, and any discussion of capital-heavy businesses, from airlines to the companies building AI data centers.
  7. The classic: "Depreciation goes up by $10. Walk me through the three statements."Possibly the single most-asked accounting question in banking and private equity first rounds.
  8. Working capital: why fast-growing companies can run out of cashAccounting technicals, credit and private equity interviews, and any question about why a growing company needs money.
  9. EBITDA: why bankers love it and why Buffett and Munger didn'tBanking, private equity and credit interviews, and any valuation question that uses EV/EBITDA.
  10. Free cash flow: unlevered vs. levered, and why investors care most about itEvery valuation discussion, and almost every DCF question.

Valuation & deals

  1. Time value of money: why $100 today beats $100 next yearThe foundation of every valuation question, and a common warm-up in wealth management and asset management interviews.
  2. Present value, discount rates, NPV and IRR: the engine behind every valuationCorporate finance and private equity interviews especially, and as a building block for every DCF question.
  3. Equity value vs. enterprise value (the house-and-mortgage analogy)Nearly every banking, private equity and equity research first round, usually as one of the first technical questions.
  4. The EV bridge: why you add debt and subtract cash (and the trick questions)Banking and private equity technicals, often as a follow-up to the enterprise value question, with a trick question attached.
  5. Valuation multiples: P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue, and which ones go togetherEvery valuation discussion, from banking technicals to "pitch me a stock" in asset management.
  6. Comparable companies analysis ("comps")Banking and equity research technicals, and any conversation about how a company or an IPO gets priced.
  7. DCF, part 1: the big idea and projecting free cash flowEvery banking, private equity and equity research process, and many asset management interviews.
  8. DCF, part 2: the discount rate (WACC, CAPM and beta)Banking and corporate finance technicals, and any question about what rising interest rates do to valuations.
  9. DCF, part 3: terminal value, and why it's most of the answerBanking and private equity technicals, often as the follow-up to "walk me through a DCF."
  10. "Walk me through a DCF": the 60-second answer and the follow-upsOne of the most common technical questions in banking and private equity, and frequent in equity research and asset management.

Markets & the economy

  1. What a stock actually is, and how the stock market worksSales and trading, asset management and equity research first rounds, and as a warm-up anywhere.
  2. Bonds 101: coupons, maturities, and why prices and yields move in opposite directionsSales and trading and asset management interviews, any markets question, and banking interviews that touch on debt.
  3. Inflation: CPI vs. PCE, and why the Fed targets 2%Markets questions in sales and trading, asset management and wealth management interviews, and "what's going on in the economy" small talk anywhere.
  4. The Federal Reserve: the fed funds rate, the FOMC and the dual mandateAlmost every markets conversation, from sales and trading to wealth management, and in "what's happening in the markets" questions.
  5. Why interest rates move stock pricesSales and trading, asset management and equity research interviews, especially "what's going on in the markets" questions.
  6. The yield curve, and what an inversion is supposed to signalSales and trading and asset management interviews, and any "what's your view on the economy" question.

Asset & wealth management

  1. Asset management vs. wealth management: what each does, and how each makes moneyEvery asset management and wealth management interview, and "why this division?" questions at big banks.
  2. Asset classes: stocks, bonds, cash, real estate and alternativesAsset management and wealth management interviews, and "how would you invest $10,000?" questions anywhere.
  3. Diversification and correlation: the only free lunch in investingAsset management and wealth management interviews, and portfolio questions in sales and trading.
  4. Active vs. passive: the index fund revolution, and why most pros trail the indexNearly every asset management interview, plus wealth management and equity research.

Investing & pitches

  1. How to pitch a stock: thesis, catalysts, valuation, risksAsset management, equity research, hedge fund and investment club interviews, and sometimes as a curveball in banking.

Trading

  1. Options 101: calls and puts in plain EnglishSales and trading interviews, especially on derivatives desks, and as a stretch question in asset management.
